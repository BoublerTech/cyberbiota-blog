About the writing

Sources and corrections

The story comes from my account of the lab and the records made while working on it.

What I use

I write from change notes, repository history, configuration records, test results, and my own account of why I started or rebuilt something. I keep the underlying private records in the lab and publish the details that help explain the work.

Dates need context

An entry’s publication date is when it was written. The events may be older. Each article has a date-and-source note at the end so a new reader can tell the difference. A folder timestamp on its own doesn’t establish when a service went live.

Describe the check that actually ran

A passing Compose check says the configuration is valid. A running container adds a startup result. A working route and a completed user workflow need their own tests. I try to keep those steps clear instead of turning one success into a claim about the whole system.

The same applies to backups. Restoring two files, restoring one database, and rebuilding the lab on replacement hardware are different exercises. When a test is synthetic or uses an offline sample, I label it that way.

Keep the gaps in the story

Some records leave questions open, including which data survived an earlier rebuild or whether a later operating-system change was an upgrade or a reinstall. I don’t fill those gaps with a guessed history. If a later result changes an earlier account, the correction needs to preserve what was known at each stage.

Keep the private details private

Public entries leave out credentials, personal records, internal addresses, and raw logs that could expose them. Diagrams and brand artwork are explanatory visuals, not screenshots offered as proof of a working system.

If you spot something that needs correcting, get in touch and point me to the entry.
