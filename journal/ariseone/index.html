AriseOne and the first repeatable jobs

Before CyberBiota, the lab was a collection of scripts. An inventory, a health check, and a ticket system gave those jobs a place to live.

Events: January to February 8, 2026Evidence: January repository records and a February retrospectiveEntry date: November 24, 2026

AriseOne is the earliest chapter for which I have both working records and a later account of how the pieces fit together. The retrospective places the first workspace setup on January 16. The January 20 repository entries supply firmer markers: health and network checks, an Ansible inventory, and system update work. The archive folder itself dates to December, but a folder can exist before any of the work inside it. January is where the story has enough evidence to start.

The health checks and inventory served different purposes. A check could capture a condition at the time it ran. An inventory gave the same job a consistent list of systems to act on. Writing update work in Ansible made the intended action easier to repeat and inspect. The early records show that direction. They do not show that all updates were unattended or that every system was covered by the same checks.

In the February 3 to 5 account, Proxmox becomes the center of the lab. Homepage gives the services a front door, Nextcloud adds file service, and Zammad becomes the place to record support work. This is also where the limitations of the setup appear. NVIDIA drivers needed attention after kernel updates. The retrospective describes a repair script and a connection to ticket triggers. I can tell that the repair was written down and that a trigger was part of the design. I can’t reconstruct a complete unattended repair from the records I have.

By February 7 and 8, the documented routine was to look in the ticket system or knowledge base before making a change. A migration moved Markdown notes into Zammad’s knowledge base. The point was practical. A recurring problem needed a history that could be searched, and a repair needed enough context to be repeated. Keeping those notes alongside the services mattered more as one change began affecting several parts of the lab.

When it came time for a fresh start, AriseOne was still part of my history. Proxmox, monitoring, and ticketing appear again in the next set of records. Their reappearance shows that the ideas persisted. The records don’t establish that every service or data set did. CyberBiota’s first dated log gives us a new starting point with a build we can follow component by component.

Reading note

Historical results describe what the records showed at the time. They are not a live status check.
