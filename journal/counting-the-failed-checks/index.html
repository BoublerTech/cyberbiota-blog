When the checks missed their target

The original failed checks and the corrected run answered different questions. Both stayed in the record.

By BoublerTechNovember 24, 20262 min read

One of my October experiments was to see how much read-only diagnostic work local assistants could handle. There were two roles and a schedule. Each role had 96 windows over a day to turn the information it was given into a diagnostic result that met the check. It was a way to test repeated work rather than rely on one good answer.

The day that missed the target

One role passed in all 96 windows. The other passed in 87. That was about 91 percent against a requirement of 95 percent. The second role had missed the target even though the first had met it. An average across both roles would have hidden where the trouble was.

After a narrow correction to the handling of diagnostic data, a shorter set of scheduled checks came back clean. The first role passed 16 out of 16, and the second passed 15 out of 15. I can use that smaller result to see that the correction helped on those runs. It isn’t another full day and doesn’t replace the failures from the first one.

The decision kept the original result in the report and allowed a limited read-only diagnostic use. It did not approve full incident handling or changes to the systems the assistants were examining. That scope matters to me because the lab still hosts my own apps and data. A read that helps me investigate a problem and a change that can affect an app are two different jobs.

I wanted both sets of numbers in the blog because they explain why the work continued with a narrow scope. Anyone reading the report later can see the missed target, the follow-up, and the part of the job I was willing to rely on at that point.
