Why I removed n8n and brought it back

The April build moved toward agent-driven work. A practical home lab article made me reconsider what a visible workflow engine was good for.

Events: April 8 and June 26–30, 2026Evidence: Dated change records and owner recollectionEntry date: November 24, 2026

n8n was removed on April 8, the day after the move to native Ubuntu. The operations log gives a reason: the new build was trying agent-driven automation in place of older workflows. The same part of the log records work on an assistant gateway and a connection to a local model. The log establishes what the rebuild was trying to do. It doesn’t tell me that every older workflow was replaced or where the original workflow data ended up.

At the time I didn’t see much use for n8n. I reconsidered after reading a Virtualization Howto article about using workflow automation and AI in a home lab. The example made it worth looking at the tool again. I don’t have a record of the exact feature I had in mind or the date I read it, so those details aren’t part of the claim. The change in my view is something I remember. Its return is also visible in the June deployment records.

The June 26 record shows n8n deployed as part of CyberBiota3. Later route checks returned HTTP 200 for the selected n8n path. That established reachability on that run. It didn’t establish that a particular workflow would receive an event, execute the right steps, or handle a failure the way I wanted.

The June 30 webhook pilot gave a more concrete test. A source service produced an event and the automation received it. The pilot had loosened transport settings to make that happen, so it was rolled back. The webhook and its workflow were removed. The underlying applications, including n8n, remained in the lab. That sequence can be hard to follow in an undated inventory. It makes sense when the removal, return, and rollback are treated as three separate events.

Bringing the tool back gave me room to test bounded workflows on their own terms. The record still leaves the earlier data disposition open. It proves the later installation and a narrow pilot, with the pilot’s reversal right beside it. That’s a more useful description of how I use tools in this lab than a list that simply marks n8n installed.
