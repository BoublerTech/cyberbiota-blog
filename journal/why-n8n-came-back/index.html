Why I removed n8n and brought it back

Removing a tool did not remove the jobs it had to do. I returned to n8n with a narrower role for it in the lab.

By BoublerTechNovember 24, 20262 min read

I didn’t think n8n was useful at first. The April 8 log shows it being removed after the move to Ubuntu. I was trying local models and agent-driven work, and the workflow engine no longer looked like something I needed to keep running.

I changed my mind after reading a home lab automation article from Virtualization Howto. That’s my recollection of what brought me back to it. I can’t point to the exact example in the article that did it. What I can show is the difference between trying a tool with no clear job for it and returning to it with a job I could test.

What came back in June

n8n appears in the June 26 CyberBiota3 deployment. A route check returned HTTP 200 that day. That told me the service answered. Four days later, the webhook pilot gave it a specific task: take an event from Gitea and hand it to a workflow. Now there was a source, a trigger and a receiver that could be checked independently.

The workflow did receive the event. The way the pilot got it there loosened the connection’s safeguards, so the webhook and associated workflow were removed and the settings were restored. Both applications stayed in the lab. This is where the April and June records can be easy to confuse. April reports removing n8n. June reports deploying it and later withdrawing one connection to it. The files I reviewed do not say whether any of the April workflow data survived.

When n8n returned, the webhook finally gave me a specific way to tell whether it was doing something useful. I could also remove that one connection without undoing the rest of the installation. The rollback has its own post.
