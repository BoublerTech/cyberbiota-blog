The rise of CyberBiota3

I wanted a fresh start for my home lab. CyberBiota3 brought together what I had learned building AriseOne and the first two versions of CyberBiota.

Events: January to November 2026Evidence: Owner recollection, historical records and dated change resultsEntry date: November 24, 2026

It was time for a fresh start in my home lab. I had already built AriseOne, and I would try two versions of CyberBiota before reaching the one I wanted to keep building. Those earlier versions taught me different things. CyberBiota3 is where I brought those lessons together.

AriseOne had started with small jobs: a health script, a list of hosts for Ansible, and a way to keep track of updates. By early February it had a Proxmox host, a file service, a dashboard, and a ticket system. The lab already had real work to do. I wanted a fresh start, and the next records show what that first CyberBiota looked like: Proxmox again, with ZFS for storage and an LXC guest bringing Docker, Ansible, and monitoring together. The records preserve both setups. They leave open exactly which AriseOne files and application data made it across.

The first CyberBiota stretched the virtualized design. The log followed a metric from Proxmox into InfluxDB and still ended the night with empty Grafana panels. Network changes added their own dependencies. After experiments with virtual gateways, I moved the lab to Ubuntu on April 7, with Docker running on the host. That became CyberBiota 2. The earlier record names Ubuntu 25.10. May records refer to 26.04, but they do not show whether I upgraded in place or rebuilt again.

By June, the CyberBiota3 records looked different. They kept the initial failure alongside the retry, separated a working route from a working application, and treated a restore as its own test. I still changed my mind about tools. I had removed n8n in April, then reconsidered it after reading about a practical homelab use for it. By June I was running it again. I also rolled back a webhook pilot that delivered its event under settings I couldn’t keep.

That is the home lab this blog follows. I want you to see what I changed, why I kept or reversed it, and how far I got before the next problem began. The chapters below follow that thread from the January scripts to the tests and operating decisions that define CyberBiota3 for me.
