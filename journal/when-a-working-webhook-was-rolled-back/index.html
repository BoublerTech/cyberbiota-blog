The webhook worked. I still rolled it back.

The n8n integration reached its target, but the trust boundary needed more work before I could keep it running.

By BoublerTechNovember 24, 20262 min read

I wanted a change in Gitea to reach an n8n workflow without somebody carrying the event between the two. The pilot proved it could make the trip. It also made the sender less picky about where it could send requests and how it checked the connection. Those were the settings in use when the event arrived. The result didn’t tell me whether the integration worked under the rules I wanted to keep.

What went back

The June 30 rollback removed the webhook at the source and the associated workflow on the receiving side. It restored the transport settings that had been widened for the pilot. The follow-up checks still showed the underlying services available. I could keep Gitea and n8n without keeping that integration.

That difference is easy to lose in a journal. Writing “n8n removed” would make it look like I had taken the service out again. Writing “webhook verified” would hide the condition I wouldn’t accept. The dated rollback records enough to name both results: event delivery was demonstrated, and that particular configuration was withdrawn.

The next time I try the connection, I want to be able to show two things with the same test setup. The intended workflow received the event, and the sender reached only the intended receiver over a connection it verified. Until those two results sit next to each other, a success message is one part of the work.
