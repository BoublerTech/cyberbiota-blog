Project history · Retrospective

The webhook worked. I still rolled it back.

The event arrived, but the pilot had changed the transport protections. The rollback removed the integration and kept the applications.

Events: June 30, 2026Evidence: Dated pilot rollback and retained service checksEntry date: November 24, 2026

The point of the pilot was straightforward. A change in a Git-backed service should reach an n8n workflow without a person carrying the event between the two. The test delivered an event. For a few minutes, the integration did what it was built to do.

The problem was in the conditions of the test. The pilot made outbound destinations more permissive and relaxed certificate verification. It proved delivery under those conditions. The lab needed an integration that could prove delivery with the intended destination and transport protections in place. Keeping the working pilot would have carried those temporary conditions into normal operation.

The June 30 rollback record gives an exact scope. The pilot webhook was deleted from the source service, and its associated workflow was removed from n8n. The temporary transport settings were reverted. The underlying applications remained available for other work. It was the integration that had failed acceptance, even though the delivery trial had succeeded.

There are at least two distinct checks in a test like this. One asks whether the intended receiver got the event and did something with it. The other asks whether the sender could reach only the intended receiver and verify the connection. A successful response can answer the delivery question without answering the trust question. The pilot record and rollback are how I can tell those two results apart now.

Later work can use this baseline without treating it as a finished replacement. Return to a narrow destination and verified connection, then repeat the delivery check. If either check fails, keep the attempt documented in its actual state. That preserves why the original test was useful and why leaving it in place would have been the wrong next step.
