Putting CyberBiota3.5 into production

The live cutover, the OpenClaw upgrade, and the tests that let me keep the new coordinator in place.

By BoublerTechNovember 25, 20264 min read

On November 25, the live coordinator delegated to a local noc-lead sandbox. When it tried to reach soc-tier1 directly, the gateway denied the request. Those two results were the last routing checks in the CyberBiota3.5 cutover.

Getting there took two production changes around a separately approved OpenClaw upgrade. Until then, the new design had been built and tested beside the live gateway.

Add the new roles and preserve the old workflows

The first cutover on November 24 began with a restricted configuration backup, a hash check, and a successful external USB backup. The merged candidate passed schema validation before it replaced the live file.

The change added 31 roles, each with its own workspace and state directory. Their settings included a Docker sandbox, no sandbox network, read-only workspace and root access, dropped capabilities, resource limits, and denied shell execution.

The existing main ID was connected to the eight department leads. Thirteen other legacy IDs, the Telegram binding, the documentation heartbeat, and the existing MCP server configuration stayed in place. That made 45 registered IDs.

The gateway took about 35 seconds to complete plugin and channel startup after the restart. Its health response and reliability monitor passed. A live governance turn identified its role and refused shell and host-file requests. Inspecting the actual sandbox confirmed its network, filesystem, capability, CPU, and memory restrictions.

The sandbox settings covered the 31 new roles; the live governance turn and container inspection sampled their runtime enforcement. The preserved coordinator and legacy roles weren’t shown to inherit the candidate’s complete policy through this additive change.

Deal with the upgrade blockers

I approved moving OpenClaw from 2026.10.4 to 2026.11.9 as its own change. The daily OpenClaw backup and USB copy had successful receipts before that step.

Candidate admission hit two blockers. An unused development symlink caused the snapshot to be rejected; its target was recorded before the link was removed. The expected policy-attestation hash also needed reconciliation with the verified post-cutover state. The policy check passed all 68 checks before proceeding.

After the upgrade, the installed version, health, schema, reliability monitor, and a sandboxed governance turn were checked again. The 37-test adapter suite passed, and the newer model entries appeared in the catalog.

One host check needed follow-up. The daily AIDE file-integrity service reported failure after the approved changes. The receipt left it for an explicit baseline review instead of reinitializing the integrity database to clear the result. That failure stayed open.

Put the hybrid coordinator on the live route

I completed device-code authorization for the production coordinator. The change didn’t copy the staging credentials into production.

main was set to openai/gpt-6.2-sol through OpenClaw’s built-in runtime, with an empty fallback list. The default remained local ollama/qwen3:8b for the other 44 registered roles.

The live smoke recorded the expected cloud model and no fallback. Credential checks found no OAuth duplication in the 31 new role directories and no OpenAI API-key environment variable in the checked gateway process.

The allowed delegation to local noc-lead ran in a restricted sandbox. The direct-specialist bypass was denied. Health, readiness, and the adapter suite passed again. I accepted the upgrade and coordinator change on November 25.

Watch the lab after the cutover

The operating handoff had been written before these changes and still called production cutover future work. The later receipts record what actually happened. They establish selected live routing and sandbox checks, alongside a separately exercised Python adapter suite.

At this entry’s date, the planned observation window ran from November 24 to December 1. It hadn’t elapsed. Daily backup receipts, stability checks, and investigation of material regressions still mattered before final acceptance.

The remaining list included the AIDE review, incomplete database dump coverage, no demonstrated WAL-based point-in-time recovery, a separately held recovery key, and a pending desktop-agent permission review. The lab still depended on one host.

The next evidence needs to come from operation: real requests through the new tools, scheduled jobs, any failures, and the eventual observation-period decision. The production change was in place; those results were still to come.
